Medicare Part B premium for 2027: $209.40 a month

The standard premium rises $6.50 on January 1, 2027. Here is what that means, who pays it, and whether your Social Security raise covers it.

The short answer

  • Standard Part B premium: $209.40 a month (up $6.50, or 3.2%)
  • Part B annual deductible: $292 (up $9 from $283)
  • Both take effect January 1, 2027

These are the official figures reported by AARP, FedRateCalc, and FedTools, citing CMS's November 17, 2026 announcement. Earlier forecasts put the premium at about $209.50. The official number is $209.40.

What Part B covers

Part B is the medical insurance part of Original Medicare. It helps pay for doctor visits, outpatient care, preventive screenings, many medical supplies, and some drugs given in a doctor's office or clinic.

Part A, the hospital insurance, is separate. Its inpatient deductible rises from $1,736 in 2026 to $1,776 in 2027.

How the premium gets paid

Most people who receive Social Security have the Part B premium taken out of their monthly benefit before it's deposited. Everyone else is billed directly. Either way, the standard amount is the same unless one of the adjustments below applies to you.

Does the Social Security raise cover it?

Social Security benefits rise 3.6% in 2027. That's the cost-of-living adjustment, or COLA. AARP reports the average retired worker's benefit rises about $75 a month.

Here is how that works for a $2,000 monthly benefit:

ItemPer month
Benefit before the raise$2,000.00
COLA raise (3.6%)+$72.00
Part B premium increase−$6.50
Net change in your check+$65.50

Because the raise is a percentage, the bigger your benefit, the more of the premium increase it covers. Try your own number on the net-check calculator.

The hold-harmless rule

If the premium increase would otherwise make your net Social Security check go down, a hold-harmless provision limits the increase so your check doesn't shrink. It generally applies only to people whose premium is deducted from a Social Security check, and it never applies to the income-related surcharge. If you think it applies to you, ask SSA.

Higher incomes pay more

People with higher incomes pay an extra monthly amount on top of the standard premium, called the income-related monthly adjustment amount, or IRMAA. It's based on the income on your tax return from two years earlier, so 2027 premiums use 2025 income.

The thresholds and amounts change every year. Check CMS's current IRMAA table before you plan around it.

Before January

  • Find your current benefit amount on your Social Security statement or in your my Social Security account. You'll need it for the net-check calculator.
  • If you have a Medicare Advantage plan or a Part D drug plan, compare your options during Open Enrollment, which runs October 15 to December 7.
  • Watch for the SSA notice showing your new 2027 benefit before it takes effect.

Common questions

Is the $209.40 premium final?

It's the figure CMS announced on November 17, 2026, as reported by AARP and FedRateCalc. The Sources page shows where each figure stands.

Will I pay the standard premium?

Many people do. You may pay more if your income is above the IRMAA thresholds, or if a late-enrollment penalty applies to you.

Does Part B cover prescription drugs?

Generally not. Most outpatient prescription drugs are covered by Part D drug plans. Part B covers some drugs given to you by a provider, such as certain infusions.

When does the new premium start?

January 1, 2027.
